  1. Delayed payouts ≠ legal escrow. Escrow depends on the licensed entity, segregated safeguarding accounts, and the exact release trigger. A “hold payout” button in an API does not create legal escrow.
  2. “Global payouts” is never one switch. Map your entity country, seller countries, settlement currencies, and KYB requirements country by country. Most providers cover parts of the corridor, not the whole chain.
  3. Headline rates ≠ real cost. FX spreads, per-payout fees, and wallet monthly fees stack on top of headline rates. The drag grows with cross-border volume.
  4. US LLC + EIN ≠ payment approval. US PayFac-as-a-Service providers and US rails often require a US-resident principal with an SSN. A Delaware/Wyoming LLC with an EIN alone will be rejected at underwriting.
  5. Migration is not an API key swap. Rebuilding webhooks, seller onboarding, reconciliation, and stored card tokens takes 4–6+ engineering weeks. Check minimum volume floors, rolling reserves, and early termination fees before signing.

Path C1

Path C1: Seller payouts, no holding, one region

If your setup is simple, staying on Stripe Connect usually wins. Full check → Pre-Migration Red Lines.

Shortlist

  • PayPal Commerce — Best for simple regional payouts where sellers already trust PayPal. Need complex multi-party splits later? Upgrade to Braintree inside the same ecosystem—but skip it for now.
  • Dodo (MoR) — Best if you don't want to touch funds at all. Dodo acts as the Merchant of Record, handling taxes and compliance for you. Limit: Digital products and SaaS only—no physical goods or offline services. Compare MoR models →

Skip & Watch-outs

Skip Mangopay, OPP, and Adyen. Wallet systems and enterprise platforms are overkill if you don't hold funds. (Note: If you might hold funds for disputes or refunds later, check Path C3 first.)

Verify before building: "Paying a creator" is not the same as supporting a marketplace. Confirm who owns disputes, what seller KYC is required, and if PayPal payouts work in your country.

Path C2

Path C2: Seller payouts, no holding, cross-continent

Shortlist

Full-stack global platforms:

  • Nuvei — Best for multi-continent sellers needing local payment methods (720+ methods, 150+ currencies, 50+ local acquiring markets). Nuvei's Payoneer acquisition (announced June 2026, closing mid-2027) will merge mass payouts into one stack. Treat Payoneer as standalone until closing and verify transition terms.
  • Airwallex — Best for multi-currency treasury and low-FX payouts. Available API features depend strictly on your incorporation country and local Airwallex license. Verify entity eligibility first.
  • PayPal Commerce — Lightweight starting point for simple global payouts. Upgrade to Braintree later for complex marketplace splitting.
  • Rapyd — Strong alternative for broad local acceptance and global payout corridors.

Regional specialists (for emerging-market seller clusters):

  • PayU Split Settlements — Dominant in Eastern Europe (Poland, Romania, Turkey), LatAm (Brazil, Colombia), India, and Africa. Splits one checkout across multiple sellers with built-in tax and commission handling.
  • Local champions — Use dLocal or EBANX (LatAm), Xendit (Southeast Asia), Razorpay (India), or Flutterwave (Africa). Verify entity, currency, and onboarding rules per country.

Payout-only add-ons (pair with your existing processor):

  • Hyperwallet or Trolley — Best for mass payouts with automated W-8/W-9 tax collection.
  • Wise Platform or Thunes — Underlying payout rails. Thunes sits at the network layer—pair it under a management layer rather than using it standalone like Trolley.

Skip & Watch-outs

Skip Mangopay and Finix. You don't need wallet holding, and Finix underwrites US/Canada platforms only.

Onboarding trap: Onboarding is a conversion funnel. Clunky foreign KYB or mandatory new wallets kill seller signups faster than high payout fees.

Next steps & real cost check

  • Map your top 5 seller corridors and verify local entity availability for Airwallex ↗, Nuvei ↗, or PayU ↗.
  • Watch the FX bleed: FX spreads across multi-continent payouts often exceed card processing fees—costing non-US platforms 1.5%–3.5% in hidden margin per cross-border dollar.

Path C3

Path C3: Seller payouts, holding required, Europe

Shortlist

  • Mangopay — Uses dedicated e-wallet architecture to hold funds for platforms and sellers.
  • Lemonway — France-licensed payment institution built for regulated marketplace fund safeguarding.
  • OPP & Ryft — Strong European alternatives for conditional fund holding and split payouts.
  • Reality check: Treat all four as legal review candidates, not automatic escrow. The real question isn't "can payouts wait?"—it's who legally holds the funds, under what license, and what triggers release.

Skip & Watch-outs

Delayed payouts ≠ escrow. Holding funds for 14 days does not create legal escrow protection. Even Stripe notes manual payouts simply leave funds in a connected balance—not an escrow service.

Skip Mollie Connect for true holding. Mollie excels at standard EU e-commerce splits (strong iDEAL and Bancontact support), but lacks heavy custody tooling.

Ask 5 legal questions before signing:

  1. Which licensed entity legally holds the money?
  2. Are user funds kept in segregated safeguarding accounts?
  3. What exact event triggers fund release?
  4. How are chargebacks and disputes handled mid-hold?
  5. Is this custody setup licensed in both your country and your sellers' countries?

Path C4

Path C4: Seller payouts, holding required, outside Europe

Shortlist

Primary full-stack candidates:

  • Airwallex — Strong marketplace payment and multi-currency holding flows. Licensing and fund-holding rules vary heavily by incorporation country—confirm regional coverage first.
  • Checkout.com — Integrated stack covering seller onboarding, acceptance, fund holding, and payouts. Best for high-volume platforms needing custom underwriting.
  • Rapyd — Broad local payment acceptance and global payout corridors. Verify whether their holding model fits your entity and release rules.

Settlement & regional add-ons (not standalone escrow):

  • Nuvei — Worth evaluating if delayed settlement or an internal wallet meets your needs (strong in LatAm and Europe). Not a substitute for legal escrow.
  • PayU & Local champions — PayU (EE, LatAm, India, Africa), dLocal/EBANX (LatAm), Xendit (SEA), Razorpay (India), and Flutterwave (Africa) handle local acceptance and splits well, but rarely offer regulated custody. Pair them with a separate custody solution.

Skip & Watch-outs

A platform API is not legal escrow. A "hold payout" button in an API does not mean funds are legally safeguarded. Never trust a sales demo—get the exact custody model in writing inside your contract.

Lock down release rules first: Document the exact trigger (delivery confirmation, service completion, or dispute window) and ask each provider what happens legally if a buyer challenges that release.

Path C5

Path C5: Partner revenue share, simple rules

Shortlist

  • PayPal — Reliable baseline for paying a predictable partner group. Best for fast recipient onboarding without building a complex marketplace stack.
  • Airwallex — Strong option for connected-account splits and low-FX international partner payouts. Verify country and API availability for both your entity and your partners.
  • Tipalti — Purpose-built for global mass partner payouts. Automates onboarding, global payout rails, and W-8/W-9 tax collection across 190+ countries.
  • Adjacent tools (for different models): Paying freelance contractors instead of rev-share partners? Use Deel's API ↗ for bundled contractor compliance. Need click/referral attribution bundled with payouts? Layer Impact ↗ or PartnerStack ↗ on top of your processor.

Skip & Watch-outs

Skip Finix and escrow wallets. Finix underwrites US/Canada platforms only, and simple scheduled commissions do not justify heavy PayFac onboarding or regulated fund holding.

Solve the refund blind spot before launch. If you pay a partner on the 1st and a customer charges back on the 10th, decide now: claw it back, net it against future payouts, or absorb the loss. Verify your provider's ledger supports that rule.

Confirm partner vs. seller status. If recipients sell directly to customers through your app, they are sub-merchants, not partners—your compliance burden changes completely.

Next steps & real cost check

  • Verify tax-form collection needs and refund-clawback rules across PayPal ↗, Airwallex ↗, or Tipalti ↗.

Shortlist

  • Braintree (PayPal Enterprise Payments) — Best if you already sit in the PayPal ecosystem and need multi-tier splits or conditional releases beyond standard PayPal Commerce.

  • Bottom line: Tiered shares, caps, and conditional splits require a scoped technical and underwriting review—not a self-serve signup.

Skip & Watch-outs

Skip basic PayPal payouts. Simple payout APIs force your engineers to build and reconcile complex ledger rules from scratch.

Don't become a PayFac just for complex splits. Use an embedded platform model instead of taking on full regulatory liability.

The US-LLC & PayFac Trap: Finix and most US PayFac-as-a-Service providers underwrite US/Canada platforms only and require a US-resident principal with an SSN. Non-US founders—even with a Delaware/Wyoming LLC and EIN—will be rejected at underwriting. Get residency requirements in writing before writing a single line of code. Never shortlist US/Canada-only providers for non-US entities.

Pre-Migration Red Lines

Pre-Migration Red Lines: Should You Leave Stripe Connect?

Stay if: Split rules are simple, sellers sit in one country, you don’t need licensed escrow, and your team cannot spare 4–6 weeks for a rebuild.

Patch first if: Cross-border payout is your only bottleneck. Keep Stripe for pay-ins and bolt on Wise, Trolley, or Payoneer for payouts. US/UK entities can also use Stripe Global Payouts; non-US/UK entities remain excluded.

Leave if: You need true legal escrow (Stripe’s manual payout delay is not escrow), cross-continent FX spreads bleed margins, or custom split logic hits Stripe API limits.

Two edge cases: Tight budget / falling volume → Don’t migrate; patch the payout layer. Zero dev capacity / pure digital goods → Switch to an MoR (Stripe Managed Payments is lowest-friction if already on Stripe, provided you drop the multi-seller model).

Contract & engineering red lines: Migration is not an API key swap—you rebuild webhooks, seller onboarding, reconciliation, and stored card tokens. Audit minimum volume floors, rolling reserves, and early termination fees before signing. Model the one-off rebuild cost →

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Stripe Connect without a US LLC?

Possibly, if your home country is supported by Stripe. Availability depends on your platform's country of incorporation, your sellers' countries, and your payout flow. Check Stripe's current supported-country list before forming a US LLC by default—especially since a US LLC alone will not bypass US-resident SSN underwriting for many US platform processors. See our Non-US Stripe Alternatives & US-LLC Underwriting Guide →

What is the difference between split payments and escrow?

Split payments route money; escrow legally holds it. Split payments determine which party receives which percentage of a transaction. Escrow is a regulated legal custody arrangement where funds are safeguarded until specific conditions are met. Simply delaying a payout via Stripe's manual payout schedule does not make it legal escrow.

How much does it cost to migrate from Stripe Connect?

There is no flat price—engineering hours and token portability drive the total cost. Rebuilding webhooks, seller onboarding, reconciliation, and stored card credentials takes several engineering weeks; see the Pre-Migration Red Lines above for the full scope.

Can I use PayPal for payouts in India?

Yes, with mandatory currency conversion limits. PayPal's payout rails allow recipients in India to receive and withdraw funds, provided your sending entity and route are approved. However, PayPal excludes holding foreign currencies in India—incoming payouts are automatically converted to INR at PayPal's FX rate before hitting the recipient's bank. Factor that conversion spread into your unit economics before building around this route.

When should I become a PayFac myself?

Only after processing volume justifies heavy regulatory and operational overhead. Becoming a registered Payment Facilitator (PayFac) requires underwriting infrastructure, compliance staffing, and sponsorship bank reserves. Complex revenue splits alone are not a reason to become a PayFac—use a PayFac-as-a-Service (PFaaS) or marketplace processor until your scale demands direct acquiring.

What is the difference between PayPal Commerce, Braintree, and PayPal Enterprise Payments?

They serve different technical tiers within PayPal's ecosystem. Following PayPal's April 2026 reorganization—which folded Braintree into PayPal's Payment Services & Crypto division under the PayPal Enterprise Payments banner—the split works like this:

  • PayPal Commerce Platform: Best for standard checkouts and simple mass payouts.
  • Braintree (PayPal Enterprise Payments): Built for full-stack acquiring, custom marketplace splits, and sub-merchant onboarding.
Why don't you list Square, Shopify Balance, or Mollie in the main shortlists?

They solve different problems or operate within narrow geographic limits:

  • Square: Operates in only eight countries and is built for single-merchant retail/online sales, not multi-party marketplace payouts.
  • Shopify Balance: A business account for US Shopify merchants to access their own store payouts faster—not a rail for paying third-party sellers.
  • Mollie: A strong European split-payment option (discussed in Path C3 watch-outs), excluded from broader shortlists only where flows require heavy regulated escrow or global coverage outside Europe.
Why isn't Finix in the C6 shortlist?

Finix underwrites US/Canada-based platforms only. Non-US founders—including US LLCs with an EIN—are rejected at underwriting, so it appears only in Watch-outs.
